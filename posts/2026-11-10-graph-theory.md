# graph theory

growing old is weird. growing old in college, specifically, i mean.

something i reflect on a lot is how much i've *truly* learned in my four years at university, especially in the ever-growing age of AI, when i know i have it easier than those before me. for example, here are a few questions i ask myself almost daily:

* do grades properly measure how much you know?
* what was the original intent of college or school in general?
* am i really applying critical thinking skills every time i open a new chat, or am i just being more efficient?

the easy answer to some of those questions, in my opinion, sits in your mindset—or how self-reflective and adaptive you are when it comes to your own intelligence. as a math and stats major, that looks like applying similar processes from my problem sets to daily life.

but after having set foot in what some might call the "real world" for the summer and working a traditional corporate 9-5, i'm left wondering how much it's all worth, how much i've learned in my almost four years that i've already forgotten, and how much i'll end up carrying some of those tough lessons that left me studying for countless hours through the rest of my life.

it's easy to view these four years as just part of the process, something you have to do to earn a respectable living wage eventually. but i want it to be worth more than that. i'm tired of reflecting on this part of my life one-dimensionally. it can be discouraging at times.

however, i wouldn't mind forgetting about graph theory now that i think about it.

maybe this is what being 22 is like.
